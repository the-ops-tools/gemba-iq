      About Gemba IQ

      Gemba IQ turns Gemba walks into decisions. One shared plant map shows where to look today, what is really happening in each sector, and whether the improvements made on the floor actually stick.

      Why it exists

      Gemba walks often end as notes on paper: problems are seen, but actions get lost, the same issue comes back on the next shift, and managers learn about it from a report days later. Gemba IQ connects four things in one place:

      1. Where to go: a daily walk plan based on risk.
      2. What is wrong: 4M status, flow against takt and live reports per sector.
      3. What we do about it: observations turned into actions with owners and due dates.
      4. Did it stick: repeat issues, improvement projects and the loop back to the standard.

      The principle behind it: fix the process first, then automate.

      Getting started

      1. Tap ☰ in the top-left corner to open the menu.
      2. Under Viewing as, choose your role: Supervisor, CI Engineer or Plant Manager. You can also tap the yellow role button in the top-right corner.
      3. Every role starts on the Plant map. Tap a sector to open its panel.
      4. Pick any other section from the menu. Only one section is shown at a time, so it works well on a phone.
      5. Tap ⓘ or About & help in the menu to come back to this page.

      Plant map and layers

      The map is the same for every role. Each rectangle is a sector of the plant. Colours follow andon logic: red = act now, amber = watch, green = OK. The sector with a thick dark border is the one selected.

      LayerWhat the number means
      Walk riskA score from 0 to 100 that says where a Gemba walk is most useful today. The yellow route numbers 1-2-3 are today's walk order.
      Flow vs taktCycle time as a % of takt time. Above 100% the sector cannot keep up with customer demand: it is the bottleneck.
      4MHow many of the four Ms (Man, Machine, Material, Method) are at risk. Each small square is one M.
      OEEOverall equipment effectiveness for the current shift.
      ImprovementsNumber of improvement projects planned, in progress or in verification in the sector.

      Sector panel: next to (or under) the map. Its tabs change with your role: Supervisor sees 4M, Events and Actions; CI Engineer sees Improvements, 4M and Events; Plant Manager sees Production, Quality, Engineering, ERP and Events.

      For every role
      Capture observation
      Anyone on a Gemba walk can record a problem: choose the area and shift, write what you saw, then Take photo (opens the phone camera) or pick one From gallery. Tap Analyse: Gemba IQ suggests the waste type, five whys and an action. Add an owner and tap Create action; the photos go with the problem into the Action plan.
      Home
      The app opens here, in the role you used last. You see today's main finding, four big buttons (Start Gemba walk, Capture, Action plan, Plant map), four numbers for your role and the list of all your sections. Switch role with the three buttons at the top.
      Bottom bar (phone)
      On a phone the main places are always at the bottom, under your thumb: Home, Map, the blue ＋ Walk in the middle to start a Gemba walk, Capture to note an observation from anywhere and Actions (the red number = late actions). All other sections are on Home. During a walk the bar changes to Back / Next, and on a phone the checklist shows one check at a time with big Yes / No / N/A buttons.
      Gemba walk
      A guided walk in 4 steps. Choose the area and the focus (Flow, Quality, Standard work, Waste, Safety, 5S, People); Gemba IQ suggests the focus from the sector data. 1 Checklist: answer each check Yes / No / N/A; every No becomes an action with an owner. 2 Observe: note a Concern, Waste, Good practice or Idea, with photos. 3 Time study (optional): time 5–10 cycles; the average can update the sector's cycle time on the map. 4 Reflect: Think (what surprised you) and Do (what you will follow up). Finishing saves a report you can print, save as PDF or copy into an e-mail. All reports are in Walk history (Home → Walk history, or the menu). Each walk counts for walk coverage and lowers the sector's walk risk.
      Process, not person
      If a text reads like blame ("operator forgot…"), a yellow note asks you to describe the process instead. Gemba looks for what in the system lets the problem happen.
      Good practice and ideas
      In Capture observation, choose the type. Concerns and waste go through Analyse to the Action plan. Good practice and ideas are saved and listed under the form, so they can be copied to other sectors.
      Action plan
      Every problem with its priority (High / Medium / Low), the responsible person (name, surname and role), the due date and photos. Open actions are sorted High first, then by due date. Gemba IQ proposes the priority: High for safety, the bottleneck sector or defects with customer complaints; Medium for other defects, waiting or problems after a change; Low for the rest. You can change it. Open shows what is still to do (late ones first); Closed shows what was done and whether the fix held. The camera button on a row adds an After photo, so before and after can be compared. Tap any photo to enlarge it.
      Action dashboard
      At the top of the Action plan: two numbers next to the title (% of actions closed on time and how many are late) and three charts. Overview: a ring chart of open actions by priority (High / Medium / Low), with how many are late. By area: a spider chart of open (blue) and late (red) actions per sector. Pareto: actions of the last 8 weeks by waste type, largest first; the blue bars are the vital few that make up about 80%. Tap a slice, a sector or a bar to filter the action list; Show all removes the filter. Each chart has a data table.
      5 golden rules
      Masaaki Imai's rules for any problem: 1 go to gemba, 2 check the gembutsu (the real thing), 3 contain it now, 4 find the root cause, 5 standardize. Each action shows 5 squares; open them to tick the real-thing check and write the containment and the root cause. An action cannot be closed without a root cause. A closed action waits for approval by the Supervisor or Plant Manager, who can approve it or reopen it.

      Plant map

      Improvement dashboard
      Active projects, projects past their due date, projects standardized and the average progress to target.
      Timeline
      Each project as a bar across the weeks, coloured by stage. The yellow line is this week. Switch between all sectors and the selected sector.
      Kanban
      Projects move through five stages: Idea (proposed), Planned (owner and dates set), In progress (being done), Verify (checking the KPI really improved), Standardized (written into the standard). Drag a card or use the arrows. On a phone, choose the column at the top.
      Waste map
      How many observations of each waste type (TIMWOODS) each sector had in the last 8 weeks. Darker = more.
      Repeat issues & root cause
      An issue counts as repeating when the same sector has the same waste type or the same trigger 3 or more times on at least 2 shifts in 8 weeks. Pick a pattern to see the evidence, what was tried before and whether it held, five whys and a hypothesis.
      Close the loop
      Fixes that worked but are not yet in the work instruction or checklist. Draft update proposes the new step; Mark updated closes it.

      How scores are calculated

      Walk risk (0–100)

      Points are added for: scrap above its 4-week average (up to 30), downtime in the last 24 h (up to 20), cycle time close to or above takt (6 or 12), changeovers today (4 each, up to 12), new operators (5 each, up to 10), an engineering change in the last 7 days (up to 15), overdue actions (4 each, up to 12) and days since the last walk when 5 or more (up to 10).

      4M status

      Man: red if fewer people than planned; amber if skills coverage is under 85% or there are new operators. Machine: red if down or availability under 80%; amber under 90% or in setup. Material: red if WIP is over its maximum; amber with a shortage or kits on time under 85%. Method: red if the standard work audit is under 75%; amber under 85%, with an open deviation or overdue actions.

      OEE

      OEE = Availability × Performance × Quality. Quality is the first pass yield.

      Gemba IQ score (0–100)

      25% walk coverage (target 4 walks per sector in 4 weeks) + 25% observations turned into actions + 30% actions closed on time + 20% issues that did not repeat. Every finished Gemba walk adds to walk coverage.

      Walk checklist score

      Yes answers divided by Yes + No answers. N/A does not count.

      Time study

      Average of the timed cycles, compared with takt. Variation = (slowest − fastest) / average; above 15% the pace is uneven (Mura).

      Data and privacy

      All plants, people, orders and numbers in this demo are fictional: Northfield Components does not exist. In a real plant, the sector reports would come live from MES, QMS, PLM and ERP.

      In the online version your changes are kept in your own browser. In the local app they are saved in the file gembaiq.db on your computer and shared with the phones on the same Wi-Fi. Reset demo data in the menu brings everything back to the start.
